Doctolib: from webpage to video

Overview

The brief was to adapt website content into a video presentation in Synthesia, using the platform's own motion tools. I chose Doctolib as the subject and made the whole piece myself, from adapting the content and sourcing assets to designing the scenes and assembling the final video.

It brought my brand design, visual communication and motion experience into a new production environment.

The challenge

A webpage and a video work differently. On a website, readers scan, skip and set their own pace. In a video, the designer decides the order. The task was to build a story viewers could follow, not just put the webpage on screen: picking the key messages, ordering them, and deciding how visuals and movement could help.

On top of that, no assets were supplied, and everything had to be built inside Synthesia.

01

Story & visuals

From page to story

I treated Doctolib's About Us content as source material to interpret rather than layouts to copy, deciding what to emphasise and how to link the messages from scene to scene so the story has a clear progression.

Building the look

With no asset kit, I gathered material from Doctolib's brand kit, recreated brand assets from scratch and pulled it all into one visual treatment, with layout, imagery and hierarchy carrying continuity between scenes. I used Synthesia's built-in AI tools to create backgrounds and avatar outfits that fit Doctolib's identity.

02

Motion & production

Designing for time

I planned how information enters the frame, where attention should go and how scenes connect, so layout, timing and movement worked together.

The motion is deliberately restrained to keep the company's story at the centre: it guides attention, introduces information and links moments, without adding visual clutter. The goal was something easy to follow, with more rhythm than a static presentation.

Made in Synthesia

Every scene layout and animation was built in Synthesia, with no external animation software. That meant developing the creative direction while learning the tool: exploring what it can do, working within its limits and making the scenes hold together as a finished piece.

Reflection

Adapting content is about more than changing its format: the structure, hierarchy and pace have to suit how the audience will experience it. The project also shows how I handle constraints: learn the tools, set a clear direction, and make purposeful choices all the way to delivery.
