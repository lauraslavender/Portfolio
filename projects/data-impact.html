Brand & web · 2020–2025

From Data Impact to NIQ

Five years leading a brand through three lives: an independent startup, a co-brand after its acquisition by NielsenIQ, and finally part of NIQ's global rebrand, without losing what made it special.

Client
Data Impact / NIQ
Years
2020–2025
Role
Brand & web designer
Scope
Brand, web, icons, templates

Overview

From 2020 to 2025 I led Data Impact's brand through every stage of its transition. It took careful visual strategy, a lot of stakeholder management, and a real commitment to keeping the original brand's spirit while lining up with new corporate identities.

01

Independent Data Impact · 2020–2021

Adding warmth

When I joined, the brand was a stark yellow and grey, with minimal, utilitarian layouts and abstract “data point” illustrations. It suited a tech-first business, but lacked the warmth clients needed to connect with it.

After benchmarking competitors, I gradually brought in a more human direction: illustrated figures, static and animated, that people could identify with, and deep blues that softened the brand while making it feel more credible. It became more engaging without losing the edge and clarity that built Data Impact's early reputation.

02

Acquisition & co-branding · 2021–2023

Finding the balance

NielsenIQ acquired Data Impact in October 2021. Our logo became a lockup with the NielsenIQ mark, and we had to present ourselves with more corporate structure, while fighting to keep the personality, voice and energy that had always defined us.

I guided the rollout of this hybrid identity carefully, making sure client-facing and internal teams had the tools they needed. It took compromise to find a place for our startup voice inside a corporate tone.

03

NIQ rebrand & full integration · 2023–2025

Coming full circle

In 2023 NIQ launched its global rebrand and, ironically, it mirrored the direction we'd already been heading in: NIQ dropped its black and green for rich blues and crisp whites. That made the next step much smoother than expected.

By 2024 we had become NIQ for Digital Commerce. I led the design templates that defined how our new identity would show up everywhere, from the NIQ website and product documentation to presentations and campaigns, and our standalone site moved into NIQ's digital ecosystem.

04

Icons that lasted

Visual language

A big part of the 2021 refresh was an icon system I created as a student designer stepping into a complex tech space. With little to draw on, I leaned into abstraction, turning e-commerce ideas like assortment, availability and product visibility into simple visual metaphors instead of literal pictures.

That early work set the tone for how we explained complex ideas clearly, even to people outside the industry. NIQ's icon library has grown since, but many of those original shapes and ideas now live on inside it, as the pairs at the top of this page show.

Challenges

Continuity: the first refresh was deliberately gradual so we wouldn't alienate longtime clients. Reliability and familiarity were non-negotiable.

Protecting the soul of the brand: the founders were adamant that Data Impact's tone, youthfulness and dynamism shouldn't be lost, and I agreed. From type choices to the tone of voice in our templates and copy, the brand team and I made sure that personality lived on under a global banner.

Outcome

This was more than a name change: it was a long-term visual and cultural integration. Through intentional, human-centred design choices, we evolved without erasing what made us unique. NIQ for Digital Commerce now has the global scale of a masterbrand, but still carries the freshness, clarity and personality of its origins, because we fought to keep it alive.
